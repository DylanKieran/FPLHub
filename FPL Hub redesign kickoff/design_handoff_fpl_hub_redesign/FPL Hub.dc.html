Empty & loading states

Five states, in the order a manager meets them
01

First run — no team connected

Team Analyser, Transfer Hub, Captain & Chips
Connect your FPL team Everything on this page is personalised to your squad — weaknesses, transfer targets, chip timing. Paste your team ID once and it's remembered.

e.g. 4127883
Where to find it Open the official site, go to Points, and copy the number in the URL after /entry/.
Browse without a team →

One card, one job. The "browse without a team" escape hatch matters — the Dashboard, Player Explorer and FDR ticker all work fine with no ID, and blocking the whole product behind an input would be wrong.

02

Loading — skeleton, pulse not sweep

Dashboard, first paint

Skeletons mirror the real layout so nothing jumps when data lands, and they pulse in place rather than sweeping a highlight across — a shimmer reads as consumer app, a pulse reads as "still fetching". Rows stagger by 80ms. Under prefers-reduced-motion the animation drops and the grey blocks simply hold.

03

No results — filters too tight

Player Explorer
Active filters GKP × Under £4.5m × Form 6+ × Reset all
No goalkeeper matches all three filters The cheapest keeper with form above 6.0 is Sels at £4.9m. Relax the price ceiling and you get 4 results.

An empty table should name the filter that emptied it and offer the single loosening that fixes it. "No players match your filters" makes the user do the diagnosis themselves.

Design notes

Assumptions, decisions, and what I'd do next

What I worked from

Every screen is a redesign of a real page in your Next.js app — Dashboard, Player Explorer, Team Analyser, Transfer Hub, Captain & Chips and Squad Builder. Content, metrics, column sets and the algorithm outputs (captain score, xPts, FDR runs, price confidence, differentials, "why this pick" breakdowns) all come from your existing pages and lib/algorithms.ts. The visual system comes from FPL_HUB_DESIGN.md.

Where I pushed past the spec

FDR cells carry the opponent, not just a number

The spec's 36×36 numeric cell reads fast but loses the fixture. Wider cells hold opponent + H/A + rating, which is the question managers actually ask.

Sidebar is one level, no collapse

Six destinations don't need a 256→64px collapse. The space went to a persistent deadline countdown instead, which is the most time-sensitive thing in the product.

Every score is paired with a sentence

Your audience is layered — casual managers want the call, serious ones want the data. So headline advice reads in plain English, and the bars/numbers sit underneath it rather than instead of it.

Charts use flat viz colours, never semantic ones

Steel/Tangerine/Mauve for series; green and red are reserved so that when they appear they always mean good and bad. The one exception is the GW-points bar chart, where colour is the judgement.

Assumptions to confirm

  • All figures are plausible placeholders for a mid-season GW24 — swap in your real API sample and the layouts hold.
  • Team badges and player photos are marked as placeholders; I haven't drawn crests.
  • A team ID is assumed connected. The empty state (no ID yet) isn't drawn yet.
  • Dark mode is shown on the Dashboard only, using the token inversion from §10 of the spec.

Next steps

  • Toast/inline patterns for optimistic actions (locking a pick, saving a squad).
  • Player compare mode — 2–3 players side by side, shared axes.
  • Squad Builder: real drag-to-swap on the pitch, and a saved-squad library.
  • A tokens file mapping this palette into your tailwind.config.ts.
